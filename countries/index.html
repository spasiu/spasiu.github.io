Handling

  • Drag a country out of the drawer and onto the globe. On a touch screen, pull it across the drawer toward the globe, or hold it for a moment and then drag it anywhere.
  • A country keeps north up wherever you carry it, so only where it goes is up to you. It sets when you let go of it near its place.
  • Drag anywhere else to turn the globe, and flick to keep it turning. North always stays up.
  • Pinch or scroll to zoom. On a trackpad, swipe sideways with two fingers to turn the globe. The arrow keys turn it too, and + and − zoom.
  • With a country in hand, a tap on the globe puts it on that spot. With nothing in hand, a tap on a set country brings up its names, capital and population. Double-tap to zoom in.
  • The drawer pictures every country to one scale that shrinks big countries more than small ones, so a bigger country always looks bigger. A magnifier beside an area marks a country drawn larger than that so you can see it.
  • Some countries have parts far from the rest (Hawaii, French Guiana, the Galápagos). They wait on the globe and fill in when their country is set.

Which countries

The 197 pieces are the 193 member states of the United Nations, its two observer states (Palestine and Vatican City), Kosovo and Taiwan. Land that belongs to no piece (Antarctica, Greenland, Western Sahara, and dependencies such as Puerto Rico or New Caledonia) is drawn in grey and is not part of the game; tap it to see its name.

About the map

Shapes from Natural Earth (1:50 million, public domain). Natural Earth draws borders as they are held on the ground; this map gives Crimea to Ukraine and the Golan Heights to Syria, as most countries do, and draws Somaliland and Northern Cyprus as part of Somalia and Cyprus.

About the names and numbers

Names and capitals in English, French and each country's own language come from Wikidata, checked by hand. Where a country has several official languages, its own language here is the most widely spoken of them. Populations are the World Bank's figures for 2025 (World Development Indicators, CC BY 4.0), except Taiwan (its Ministry of the Interior, end of 2025) and Vatican City (the Vatican's own count of residents).
