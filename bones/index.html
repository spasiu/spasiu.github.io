    Handling

    • Drag a bone out of the drawer and into the body. On a touch screen, pull it across the drawer toward the body, or hold it for a moment and then drag it anywhere.
    • The drawer pictures each region's bones to one scale, so a big bone looks big. A magnifier beside a size marks a bone drawn larger so you can see it.
    • A bone turns itself to fit the body as it leaves the drawer, so only where it goes is up to you.
    • To look at the bone in hand from any side, turn it in the round viewer: drag inside to tumble it, drag the rim to spin it, double-tap to turn it back. The arrow keys and [ ] do the same. This never changes how it sits in the body.
    • Drag anywhere to turn the body, and flick to keep it turning. It stays upright however far you go.
    • Pinch or scroll to zoom. On a trackpad, swipe sideways with two fingers to turn the body.
    • To slide the body across the view, drag with two fingers on a touch screen, or with the right button, or hold Space and drag. To roll the view, twist two fingers or hold Alt and drag.
    • With a bone in hand, a tap on the body puts it on that spot. With nothing in hand, a tap on a set bone names it. Double-tap to zoom in.
    • Open About this bone, under the name of the bone in hand, for a short description and a link to its Wikipedia article.

    Muscles

    • Switch to Muscles at the top to dress the finished skeleton. Each muscle has ends to attach: its origins and its insertions, one for every head or tendon.
    • Pull a muscle out of the drawer and it comes out held by one end, in the pose it has in the body, its belly firm and its ends loose. Take that end to the spot on the bone where it attaches. When it is on the spot the spot is named; let go and it stays on, and the muscle droops from it.
    • Each end still loose has a handle, red for an origin and blue for an insertion. Drag a handle to its spot to attach that end. The head it belongs to lights up while you hold it. Dragging the muscle by anything else just moves it about. Once every end is on, the muscle settles into its shape.
    • The panel under its name lists each end, the head it belongs to and where it goes, and About this muscle lists its heads. On Relaxed, the spots for the muscle in hand are marked on the bones in the same colours. Show me where marks them on any setting.
    • The drawer draws longer muscles larger, but not all to one scale, so that small ones can still be seen. The number under each is its real length.

    Organs

    • Switch to Organs at the top to fill the body. The skeleton turns see-through, so you can work inside the ribcage and the pelvis. The brain comes by lobes, the lungs by lobes, the heart by chambers and the gut by its parts, along with the glands, the kidneys and bladder, the great vessels and the reproductive organs of the body chosen under Body.
    • Each organ has one to three connections: where it joins the next part (the stomach meets the duodenum at the pylorus), a valve, or the hilum where its vessels enter. Each has a handle in its own colour. Drag a handle to where it joins; when it is there the place is named, and when you let go it stays joined.
    • A connection carried close to its spot on screen drops to that spot's depth, so you can join it from any angle.
    • Organs keep their shape, so once one connection is made the organ hangs where it belongs, and each of its other connections that comes to rest on its spot joins by itself. With every connection made, the organ settles into place. One left off its spot (on Expert, say) still has its handle: drag it there.
    • The panel lists each connection in its colour. On Relaxed, the spots for the organ in hand glow in the same colours, wherever they are in the body. Show me where marks them on any setting.

    Seeing through

    • See-through, next to the drawer's tabs, makes everything from the tab showing see-through once it is in place, so you can see what is behind and inside. On All it does the whole mode. Press it again to make them solid.
    • To do one at a time, tap something in place and press See-through in its panel.
    • In Muscles and Organs, Bones does the same for the skeleton.
    • A tap on something see-through picks whatever is behind it.

    The two bodies

    • Under Body, Female gives every mode a woman's hip bones, sacrum and coccyx fitted to the same skeleton, the muscles on them, her outline, and her bladder and reproductive organs. What is in place stays in place when you switch.
    • The skeleton is a man's; only the pelvis changes. The vagina and vulva are not modelled.

    About the models

    Bone and body shapes: BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International. The meshes were simplified for the web, the coccyx was separated from the sacrum, teeth are attached to their jaws, and the body surface was smoothed into a neutral figure and eased outward by up to 4 mm where a bone would otherwise touch it. Bones are life-size relative to the body. Drawer pictures are to scale within each region.

    The six ear ossicles are not part of that dataset. The ones here are simplified stand-ins modelled for this app.

    About the female body

    The woman's hip bones, sacrum and coccyx, the hips and breasts of her outline, and her uterus, uterine tubes, ovaries, bladder and mammary glands come from the Human Reference Atlas 3D Reference Object Library (HuBMAP), licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International, made from the Visible Human Project female dataset of the U.S. National Library of Medicine. Her pelvis was fitted to this skeleton's hip joints and lowest vertebra, keeping its shape, and scaled with them; her organs keep their place in it. The muscles on the pelvis were carried onto hers, and the man's outline was eased over his genitals.

    About the descriptions

    Each bone's description is a short summary written for this app from its article on the English Wikipedia, which the link under it opens.

    About the muscle models

    Muscle shapes come from BodyParts3D (CC Attribution 4.0) and, for the muscles BodyParts3D does not model (masseter, temporalis, the pterygoids, latissimus dorsi, rectus abdominis, internal oblique, transversus abdominis, quadratus lumborum, multifidus, extensor digitorum brevis and the dorsal interossei of the foot), from Z-Anatomy, the libre 3D atlas of anatomy by Gauthier Kervyn, fitted onto this skeleton. The muscle models as adapted here are licensed under CC Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0. Where each muscle attaches was found from where it touches its bones, checked against standard anatomy. Each muscle's description was written for this app; the link under it opens its article on the English Wikipedia.

    About the organ models

    Organ shapes come from BodyParts3D (CC Attribution 4.0) and, for what BodyParts3D does not model (the lobes of the lungs, the chambers of the heart, the lobes of the cerebrum, the parotid and thyroid glands), from Z-Anatomy, the libre 3D atlas of anatomy by Gauthier Kervyn, fitted onto this skeleton, as is the ductus deferens. The woman's organs are from the Human Reference Atlas, as above. The cecum and the sigmoid colon were cut from BodyParts3D's ascending and descending colon. The organ models as adapted here are licensed under CC Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0. Where each connection goes was found from where the organ meets its neighbour, checked against standard anatomy. Each organ's description was written for this app; the link under it opens its article on the English Wikipedia.
