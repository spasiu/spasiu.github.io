This page is a template, and the text below is advice for writing a case study rather than one. A case study reads well in four parts: the situation, what you built, what changed, and what you’d do differently.

The situation

Say who the customer was and what was hard for them, in their terms rather than yours. One paragraph is usually enough. If you can’t name the client, describe them: “a US insurer with 400 agents” tells a reader more than a logo would.

What I built

Describe the shape of the solution and the decisions that mattered. A diagram helps. The one below is plain SVG in the page, drawn with the site’s colours, so it follows the lights and stays sharp at any size. Edit the boxes and labels in the HTML.

Decisions worth explaining

  • Why you chose one service or pattern over another.
  • What you left out on purpose, and why.
  • Where the risk was, and how you reduced it.

What changed

Give numbers if you have them, with a time frame: “after-call work fell from four minutes to under one in the first month.” If you don’t have numbers, describe what people stopped having to do.

Add a short quote from someone you worked with, with their permission, and credit them underneath.

What I’d do differently

One honest paragraph. It shows judgment, and readers who hire tend to read this part closely.
